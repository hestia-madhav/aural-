## 1. Program Context

The English Proficiency Program (EPP) uses three tools to help children in rural Karnataka learn spoken English:

1. **Bilingual Storybook** — "Kaadina Shaale" (physical book with English text and Kannada transliteration)
2. **Aural Device** — audio playback device for listen-and-repeat practice
3. **Digital Storybook** — web version of the same story with integrated audio

This is the first formal **field trial** session. Prior to this, ~12 weeks of **field testing** sessions were conducted at various libraries to collect data on the Aural device. Reports from those earlier sessions exist separately and will be linked/consolidated.

**Pilot locations for the 8-week trial cycle:**
- Akkuru (this session)
- Nila Sandra
- Tagikupe
- Sogala

**Goal:** Collect baseline and midline data over 8 weeks to measure whether the EPP program measurably improves children's English speaking ability.

## 3. Session Flow

1. **Orientation & Introduction** — Explained the EPP program to the children
2. **Storybook Distribution** — Gave out Kaadina Shaale books
3. **Reading Phase** — Two batches of ~5 children each read through all four quadrants of the story
4. **Aural Device Introduction** — Introduced the device after reading was complete
5. **Read & Repeat** — Children used the device alongside the storybook
6. **Extended Practice** — Kids got excited and spontaneously started doing "oral tests" — reading passages and reciting them back to the facilitator
7. **Baseline Video Capture** — Recorded individual children reading

**Note:** The session extended well beyond the planned duration because children were highly engaged. Interest tapered around the 2-hour mark, which is expected for this age group.

---

## 4. Observations

### 4.1 Engagement with the Storybook

- **High engagement overall.** Children were genuinely interested in the story and the pictures.
- The **Kannada transliteration of English** (English words written in Kannada script) was the standout feature — kids described it as a "cheat code" because they could start speaking English directly without decoding English script.
- All children liked the characters. **Leo** and **the monkey** were the most popular.
- Children kept asking "when is the next time?" — strong interest in continuing.

### 4.2 Readability

| Quadrant / Format | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Regular Kannada | Easiest |
| English in Kannada script (transliteration) | Easy — this is what kids loved most |
| Kannada in English script (romanized Kannada) | Difficult — kids struggled |
| Original English sentences | Most difficult |

**Key insight:** Some English words don't transliterate cleanly into Kannada. The English sentence selection could be improved to favour words that sound/read more naturally when transliterated.

### 4.3 Difficult Areas

- **Vocabulary:** Unfamiliar words like "listen"
- **Grammar/Narration:** Third-person storytelling voice ("he said", "she said") was conceptually difficult — children are not accustomed to reading a story told *about* someone versus direct speech
- The difficulty was less about individual words and more about the **narrative structure**

### 4.4 Aural Device Usage

- **Intuitive use:** Children figured out how to use the device without needing instruction
- **Read-and-repeat behaviour:** Strong — children were repeating both English and Kannada sentences
- **Multi-modal engagement:** Some children had the storybook open alongside the Aural device, reading while listening
- **Enthusiasm:** Almost all children were very positive — said it makes learning English easy. The device + book combination was well received.

## 6. Logistics & Recommendations for Next Session

### What Worked
- Library setting was supportive and welcoming
- Sunita's co-facilitation was very helpful
- Two-batch reading approach (groups of ~5) worked well
- Baseline video capture process was smooth

### What Needs to Change
- **More books needed.** Books are the single biggest enabler of individual-level learning. The device can be shared between children, but each child benefits from having their own book. Printing more copies is the top priority.
- **Session length:** Core session should target 1 hr 15 min. Children's focus drops significantly after ~2 hours.

### Content Improvements to Consider
- Review English sentence selection — choose words that transliterate more naturally into Kannada script
- Consider simplifying third-person narration or adding a brief orientation on storytelling voice before reading begins
